# Privacy Policy — StackLab

**Last updated: November 10, 2026**

StackLab is a precious-metals collection tracker developed by StackWar. This policy describes what the app does with your data — and above all, what it doesn't.

## The essentials, in five sentences

- **Your collection stays on your phone.** StackLab has no account system, no server storing your collection, no cloud sync.
- **No usage analytics, no trackers, no ads, no sale of data.**
- **The app contacts two services:** our gold and silver price service, which receives nothing about you; and RevenueCat, the provider that validates Shop purchases, which receives an **anonymous technical identifier** and your **purchase data** — never your collection.
- **Payment is handled by Apple or Google.** StackLab never receives your card number, bank details, or your name.
- **Your backups are encrypted**, and only leave your device when you share them yourself.

## What the app stores, on your device only

- **Your collection**: items, purchase prices, quantities, dates, notes, organisation into labs and cases. Stored in a local database inside the app's private storage.
- **Your item photos**: saved in the app's private storage. They are never uploaded anywhere, and are never analysed or run through image recognition — photo framing happens entirely on the device.
- **Your PIN** (if you enable App Lock) and **your backup keys**: kept in the system's secure storage — on Android, protected using Android Keystore; on iPhone, in the keychain. The app never sends them anywhere. Your backup keys are marked "this device only": never synced to any cloud, never included in a backup. On iPhone, in version 1.0.0 (12), your PIN does not carry that mark: an iCloud backup, or an encrypted computer backup, can carry it along with the app's data, encrypted by Apple.
- **Your settings**: currency, weight unit, language, backup preferences.
- **Your access rights** (Free, Silver, Gold, Ambassador): a local copy of what you own, so the app works offline. It is never included in a backup and never travels in a file.
- **Public price history**: daily gold and silver prices, identical for everyone. This is not personal data — it says nothing about what you own.

## What travels over the network

### 1. Metal prices

The app queries our price service for current and historical gold and silver prices. These requests:

- contain **no identifiers** — not of you, your device, or your collection;
- like any internet connection, make your **IP address** technically visible to the service. It uses it only to limit the number of requests per device, in memory, and forgets it after ten minutes of inactivity; it logs neither your address nor the content of your requests. The service is hosted on Render (United States), which may keep its own connection logs under its own policy;
- travel over HTTPS.

If you block all connectivity, the app remains usable — only live prices become unavailable.

### 2. Shop purchases — RevenueCat

Since version 1.0.0 (build 12), StackLab offers in-app purchases (Silver, later Gold). To validate these purchases and find them again after a reinstall, the app uses **RevenueCat** (RevenueCat, Inc., United States), a specialised provider acting on our behalf and on our instructions.

**What RevenueCat receives, from the first launch of the app:**

- an **anonymous technical identifier**, generated by RevenueCat's software on first launch, specific to this installation and unrelated to your name, email, or store account. You can read and copy it in the Shop;
- your **purchase data**: identifiers of purchased products, the Apple receipt or Google purchase token that RevenueCat verifies with the store, transaction price and currency, date, status (active, refunded);
- **technical information**: platform and OS version, app version, device language and currency;
- your **IP address**, technically visible on each call like any connection. When the transaction country is unavailable, RevenueCat may use the IP address to estimate the country and states that it does not retain the address after that determination.

**What RevenueCat never receives:** your collection, your items, their quantities and purchase prices, your photos, your labs and cases, your dashboard, your backups, your PIN, your name, your email, your phone number, any advertising identifier. We enable no attribution, marketing, or device-identifier collection option.

**What it is used for:** running the Shop (proving a purchase, activating your rights, restoring them on a new device, removing a refunded right); preventing fraud; and giving us **aggregated sales statistics** (number of purchases, revenue), with no individual profile. Legal basis: performance of the purchase contract and our legitimate interest in securing sales.

**Where and for how long:** RevenueCat hosts this data in the United States, under its data processing agreement. It is kept as long as your anonymous record exists at RevenueCat; you can ask for its deletion (see below).

**Codes and benefits:** a promotional code is redeemed with Apple or Google, under their own terms; StackLab only receives the resulting right. A benefit we grant directly (for example during the beta) is attached to your anonymous technical identifier: the store does not restore it, and a reinstall loses it.

### 3. Apple and Google

The payment itself — card, billing, refunds, named receipt — is handled by the App Store or Google Play, under their own privacy policies. **StackLab never receives your bank details, your name, or the email address of your store account.** A purchase comes back with "Restore purchases" on the same platform and the same store account; an Apple purchase is not valid on Android, and vice versa.

### What does not travel

No account, no sync, no usage analytics tool, no automatic crash reporting, no ads. If that ever changes, it will be stated here **before** activation, and will never include collection data.

## Your backups

- Automatic backups stay **inside the app's private storage**, on your device.
- Copies you export are **encrypted** with keys generated randomly on your device (ChaCha20-Poly1305 for a full copy, AES-256-GCM for a data-only copy). You alone hold the recovery kit that can open them elsewhere.
- A copy only leaves the device through **your explicit share action**, to a destination you choose. What that destination (for example your personal cloud storage) does with the copy is governed by its own policy, not ours.
- **On Android**, StackLab is **excluded from cloud backup and from automatic device-to-device transfer**: nothing goes to Google Drive without your knowledge.
- **On iPhone**, that exclusion does not exist: if you have iCloud Backup enabled, it takes the app's data along with your other apps', encrypted by Apple. Your backup keys are excluded from it; in version 1.0.0 (12), your PIN is not.
- **Your photos can travel with your backup.** At export you choose: a full copy, photos included, or a light copy, data only. Before any restore, the app tells you what the file contains. Photos travel encrypted, inside the same file, and never pass through any server.
- **Your access rights are not in the backup**: they come back through "Restore purchases", not through a file.

## What we don't do

- No user accounts, no sign-up.
- No usage data collection, no analytics tools, no advertising trackers, no cross-app tracking.
- No selling or sharing of data for commercial purposes. RevenueCat, Apple and Google receive only what is described above, to make purchases work.
- No automatic crash reporting in the current version.

Note: as with any app distributed through Google Play or the App Store, Google and Apple may provide us with **aggregated, anonymous statistics** (installs, crashes) from devices whose users have opted into sharing that data with them. We have no access to anything individual.

## Permissions

- **Camera / photos**: requested only at the moment you add a photo, never before. Declining shows a clear message and blocks nothing else.
- **Face ID / fingerprint**: requested only if you choose to unlock the app with something other than your PIN. The check is performed by the system; the app only receives the result.

## If you write to us

When you contact us (email, the beta Discord), we receive what you send: your address or nickname, your message, and, if you attach them, your anonymous technical identifier and an incident code. We use this information only to handle your request. The app can prepare an email draft with the incident code, the identifier and the app version: **you review and send it yourself**, and it never contains collection data.

**Beta programme.** If you send us your anonymous technical identifier to receive a benefit, we note it in a minimal private register (nickname or contact, platform, identifier, date), kept by the publisher alone, never published or transferred. It is erased at the latest when the beta programme closes. Share this identifier in a private message, never in a public channel. Discord applies its own privacy policy.

## Deleting your data, and your rights

Your data is yours, and so is its deletion:

- from settings, **"Delete everything"** erases your collection: labs, cases, items, value snapshots, and photos. Your backup keys and your recovery kit are **deliberately kept**, so that copies you have already exported remain openable. Your purchases are untouched: they belong to you;
- **disabling App Lock** immediately disables protection in the app. In version 1.0.0 (12), this action does not delete the PIN from the system's secure storage;
- **automatic wipe**, if you enabled it, goes further: after too many wrong PIN attempts, it also destroys the backup keys, the recovery kit, and the backups held on the device;
- **uninstalling the app** erases its local data, including the local copy of your rights and the anonymous technical identifier. On iPhone, the PIN and the keys do however remain in the phone's keychain — that is how iOS behaves for any app. Your purchases come back with "Restore purchases";
- **your purchase data at RevenueCat**: write to us with your anonymous technical identifier (shown in the Shop) and we will request its deletion at RevenueCat. This deletion erases the anonymous record and its history at RevenueCat; it cancels no purchase with Apple or Google, and a purchase remains restorable through your store account. For a refund, contact Apple or Google;
- there is **no server-side copy of your collection** — we cannot see it, so we cannot retain it.

You have the right to access, rectify, erase and object to the processing of data concerning you, under the General Data Protection Regulation. You may also lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority of your country (in France, the CNIL).
